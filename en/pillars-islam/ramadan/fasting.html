Fasting in Islam

Fasting is an act of worship dedicated to Allah which consists in abstinence from all things that invalidate it. The period of a day of fasting extends from dawn until sunset, and not from sunrise to sunset.

As-siyam, fasting in Islam

There are 4 types of fasting:

1) Fasting compulsory: fast of the month of Ramadan , of the atonement or of the one who s commit to fasting for a certain number of days.

2) Fasting prohibited: fasting for the 2 days of celebrations (aid), fasting for the 3 days of tachriq and fasting for women uncomfortable with menstruation or lochia.

3) Fasting recommended: Monday and Thursday, the 6 days of Shawwal, days of the month of Sha'ban, the first 9 days of dhou al-hidjah, the fast of Ashoura.

4) Fasting not recommended: fasting on Friday only, the day of doubt.

People who have to fast

Fasting is compulsory for all Muslims who have reached the age of puberty, holy of mind (who is not mad), who is resident (not a traveler) and who is physically capable and resident (not a traveler).

People exempt from fasting

He who is unable to fast because of old age, or a chronic and incurable disease, and who is obliged to feed a poor person every day missed. Also, the patient whose hope for recovery does not fast if it is painful. He will compensate after his recovery with a number of days equivalent to those missed.

For pregnant and breastfeeding women, if fasting is painful for them, or for fear for the health of their child, may not fast. They will compensate (according to the majority opinion), the days missed once the fast will have become easier for them.

Women who are uncomfortable with menstruation or lochia are not allowed to fast. They are required to make up for the days missed after their indisposition period.

The traveler, on the other hand, can choose between fasting or not. If he does not fast, he must make up for the days missed. In a hadith reported by Muslim, according to Abu Said al-Khudri: "when we went to jihad, some of us fasted, but we did not criticize each other. Those who felt capable of fasting found it more advantageous to do so, those who were unable to do so considered it more beneficial to break the fast ”

What is recommended during fasting

  1. Eat a meal before dawn (as-souhour) as late as possible
  2. Brushing your teeth with the siwak
  3. Hasten the break from fasting at sunset
  4. Break the fast with dates, otherwise water
  5. Invoke Allah by breaking his fast
  6. Get away from sins and what leads to them
  7. Increase generosity and kindness
  8. Read and review the Koran
  9. Stay up at night in prayer

What cancels fasting has 2 consequences

We speak of al-qada, which is the compensation of a day for each day missed or invalidated. There is also al-qada and kaffara, a compensation and an atonement.

For catching up (al-qada)

  1. Eat or drink voluntarily
  2. Vomiting voluntarily
  3. Voluntarily swallowing what is not edible
  4. Decide to break the fast (even if the person does not break it)
  5. Voluntary ejaculation

Catching up and atonement (al-qada wa al-kaffara)

One who has had sex with his spouse on a day of Ramadan must, in addition to the compensation for the disabled day, choose between:

  1. Free a servant (this is no longer the case)
  2. Fast 2 consecutive months
  3. Feed 60 poor people

Anyone who has not been able to catch up on their days before the arrival of the following Ramadan is obliged to do so after Ramadan, with the additional obligation of feeding the poor for each missed day. The catch-up can be spread over the whole year. However, it is advisable to start by catching up on the compulsory Ramadan fast before performing the supererogatory fast. Finally, it is recommended for the family of the deceased who could not catch up, to do it in their place, or to give alms.
